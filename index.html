Hello — I’m Blase.

I build hardware, then try to break it.

I’m a mechanical engineering undergraduate at UC San Diego, graduating in 2027 and staying on for a master’s. I’ve worked across most of the stack — CAD, composites, PCB design, MATLAB, machining and motor control — and I like the part of a project where the drawing stops being a drawing.

So far that has meant instrumentation hardware for a full-scale wind tunnel at NASA Ames, a CAN bus motor control network in Professor Gravish’s bioinspired robotics lab, and composite wheel hubs and a compliant gripper for UCSD’s Mars rover team. I’m always looking for environments where I can build fast and test to failure.

A few of those are below. The projects page has all ten, and my résumé is here as well.

Selected work

  • NFAC Transducer Adaptor PCB

    A low-cost KiCad board that fixes a signal-clipping condition on decades-old strain gauge amplifiers, recovering roughly two channels per board across a NASA wind tunnel data acquisition system.

    KiCad · NASA Ames · 2026

  • Rover Fin-Ray End Effector

    A compliant TPU gripper whose ribs buckle in a controlled way so each finger wraps around whatever it touches. Fielded on the 2026 competition rover.

    TPU · ASA · Yonder Dynamics · 2026

  • Thermoacoustic Refrigerator

    A heat pump driven by a standing wave — no compressor, no refrigerant, no moving parts. Led a three-person team and reached a steady-state 4°C drop.

    ANSYS Fluent · Onshape · IEEE-HKN · 2026

  • Carbon Fiber Wheel Hubs

    A three-ply vacuum-bag wet layup over a wood-lathed mould, CNC post-processed. Half the weight of the printed hubs they replaced.

    Composites · Onshape · Tormach · 2025
